In everything I've done, in every goal I've achieved in my life (whether at work, in sports, or in relationships), I've always tried to go beyond the limits imposed by the "average." By "average" I don't mean mediocrity, but rather a way of doing something well enough to stay and compete, but not enough to excel. This has always made me angry with myself! I've always looked for that small detail to refine: a way of executing, thinking, and preparing. It still torments me today, but at the same time, it fuels me to keep going. To avoid letting this become toxic and kill my mood, I've spent quite a bit of time reflecting on it over the past few months.

I've tried several approaches with little success, except for one practice I'd like to call _pragmatic gratitude_: writing down in a journal what I've done well and what I'm grateful for, while also identifying the next small step to take to move forward. Let's look at a concrete example. I started playing tennis exactly one month ago. My backhand comes fairly naturally to me, but my forehand doesn't. Instead of obsessing over every detail, starting advanced training programs, driving myself crazy, and turning a new sport into a burden rather than something fun, I simply did this: I'm grateful that I can hit a good backhand, and I'm grateful that I've understood and internalized the forehand motion; next time, I need to try to rotate my upper body better and not get too close to the ball. That's it. Sure, I still need to work on my footwork, my swing, my wrist... a million things. But I've only been playing for a month, and that's perfectly fine.

I find this a common pattern in everyday life. We should _learn again how to learn_. Especially in the age of AI, social media, and hyperconnectivity, where everyone puts on a show and presents themselves as knowledgeable, dutiful, perfectionist, talented. We need to take the time to embrace our humanity, which includes making mistakes and requires time for learning and understanding. We should not be passively looking for shortcuts, but actively reflecting on ourselves and our next steps, rather than on some ultimate guide that will make us perfect (spoiler: it doesn't and will never exist!).
