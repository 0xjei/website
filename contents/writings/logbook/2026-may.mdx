I recently started taking tennis lessons. After achieving good goals and results in basketball, I felt the need to challenge myself with an individual sport. Since I am not a fan of combat sports, I chose between running and tennis. I have been running for a year now at a good pace (an average of under 5 minutes per kilometer over 15 km), and I think I will run my first half marathon next year, trying to maintain a steady pace for the entire 21 km. But how did I get here?

Last January, I weighed about 100 kg (I am 184 cm). I had never been so out of shape. Since I could not play basketball at the time (which I restarted last September), I decided to take up running combined with a healthy diet. I never quit the diet since that date! On my first run, I went out wearing a pair of shoes I had bought two years earlier and never used, along with a Nike jacket, basketball shorts, and wired headphones that I still use today. I ran 3 km and felt completely wiped out, utterly exhausted. I still remember the pace: 8 minutes per kilometer. I was exhausted, yes, but not physically… mentally. I felt uncomfortable; I saw myself as unattractive, slow, clumsy, and not dressed properly. I saw others on the course running calmly, smiling, happy. They all looked like pros to me. Then, after two months, I managed to run my first 7 km at 6:30. Then 10 km, then 12 km, then 5:45, 5:30; and now I run under 5 minutes with relative ease. Now I have my Asics Novablast 5, proper running gear, and everything else. But it took me a year and a half of sacrifices after work, running in the rain, lifting weights, and sleepless nights due to joint pain to get here. With basketball, it took about a month, and by combining running and the gym, I reached a good level. Having worked on my technique for years, the only thing missing was the physical conditioning.

Right now, I am in the prime of my physical life. To be honest, I have never felt better! But tennis has brought me back to this uncomfortable situation. And I think it is actually a good thing, even though it is hard to deal with. Why? Because I think no one in the modern world is used to this feeling anymore. I open the few social media platforms I still use occasionally, and everyone seems to know everything, is impolite, and acts mostly superior. I read the data and analyses on social dynamics and relationships, and I see a mass of people who are not used to **discomfort**: who, at the first sign of trouble, switch jobs as if choosing from an endless catalog, or ghost their partners. People change jobs at the first sign of trouble and now, with AI, we avoid that trouble even more. It is as if we are all standing in front of a rickety bridge but will not cross it because, perhaps, a new one will be built soon. Maybe I will not be able to wrap up a project in six months, but who knows… maybe in one, two, or five years, it does not matter. I will win my first semi professional tournament. Or maybe I will come in second, enjoying the journey of having made it that far and, of course, preparing for the next one ;)
